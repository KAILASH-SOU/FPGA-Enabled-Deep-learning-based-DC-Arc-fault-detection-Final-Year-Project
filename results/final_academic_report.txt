1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROBLEM STATEMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AC arc faults pose severe fire and explosion risks in electrical distribution networks.
Traditional thermal-magnetic circuit breakers fail to detect arcing because fault currents
often do not exceed nominal overcurrent thresholds. Deep learning 1D-CNN models provide
superior pattern recognition across combined time-frequency (FFT) domains. However,
deploying floating-point 32-bit (FP32) neural networks to edge hardware (FPGAs) introduces
prohibitive hardware costs in DSP slice count, memory bandwidth, and power consumption.

This report documents the experimental results obtained from training and evaluating
the 1D-CNN architecture on 100,000 real AC current samples (8,000 Hz, 160-point window).
We contrast the 32-bit floating point baseline against Post-Training Quantization (PTQ)
and Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) targeted at Xilinx Artix-7 / Zynq-7000 FPGAs.

3. QUANTIZATION-AWARE TRAINING (QAT) VS POST-TRAINING QUANTIZATION (PTQ)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Post-Training Quantization (PTQ): Rounds pre-trained FP32 weights to 8-bit fixed-point (INT8).
  While fast, PTQ introduces rounding errors because weights were never optimized for discrete
  fixed-point grids, resulting in noticeable accuracy drops and elevated False Alarm Rates.

- Quantization-Aware Training (QAT): Simulates 8-bit fixed-point clamping and rounding during
  the forward training pass using Straight-Through Estimators (STE). The gradient descent
  adapts the network weights to overcome quantization noise, fully recovering baseline accuracy
  while operating with an 8-bit integer budget.
